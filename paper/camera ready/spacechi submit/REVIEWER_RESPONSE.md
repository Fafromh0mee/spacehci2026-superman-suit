# Response to reviewers

We thank both reviewers for their constructive assessments. The revision sharpens the paper's scope and HCI contribution while preserving its position-paper and theory-bearing-artifact framing.

## Reviewer 1

1. **Physiological target and outage boundary.** We now define the safety net's target as _activation continuity_: retaining the capability to complete one preauthorized, user-initiated lower-limb NMES session during a 24--72-hour interruption. We distinguish electrical delivery from the future physiological endpoint of measurable evoked force or torque and explicitly exclude exercise equivalence and demonstrated protection from deconditioning.

2. **Rationale for integration and meaning of visual choices.** The System Architecture section now explains what integration uniquely contributes: shared resource-to-body accounting, co-located uncertainty and contact data, one user-governed actuation interface, and a common fail-safe state. It also names the costs of integration and treats a photovoltaic overshirt, removable storage, and separately certified NMES underlayer as a serious modular baseline. The text distinguishes four theory-relevant placement principles from the exact panel, electrode, sensor, and stop geometry, which remains illustrative.

3. **Computational probe.** The main results table now foregrounds transitions among autonomy-candidate, conditional, assisted, and unavailable states, alongside sensitivity drivers. Exact model-internal probabilities remain in the supplement and are explicitly described as non-empirical because the distributions are illustrative.

4. **Distinctive HCI contribution.** The Discussion now states the central interaction claim directly: when uncertain resource estimates govern bodily actuation, provenance is part of informed action. The interface separates observation, recommendation, decision, and actuation, and the paper strengthens its treatment of affirmative initiation, refusal, local stop, and post-action accounting.

5. **Editorial and bibliography pass.** Draft-remnant language was removed. Reference 6 now uses the correct first author, full author list, issue, and page range. Reference 12 is now correctly led by T. R. Macaulay rather than M. Mortreux.

## Reviewer 2

1. **Operational scenarios.** The Introduction now bounds use to short, recoverable interruptions such as 24--72 hours of exercise-device inspection or repair, a missed exercise period during temporary rover or safe-haven isolation, or a short habitat power-allocation interruption. Medical contraindication, loss of authorization, long-duration outage, and habitat emergency are explicit non-use cases.

2. **User needs, astronaut experience, and mission-life integration.** A new subsection walks through retrieval, module connection, fit/contact checks, preview, initiation or refusal, post-session reporting, cleaning, inspection, and stowage. It states that no astronaut has evaluated the concept and makes participatory work with astronauts, flight surgeons, exercise specialists, and maintainers Stage 0 of the research agenda. Acceptance factors now include fit, heat, moisture, mobility, skin tolerance, privacy, visibility, alarm burden, cleaning, and repair.

3. **Difference from existing solutions.** The related argument now distinguishes the contribution from existing EMS garments, photovoltaic textiles, and smart textiles. The novelty claimed is their HCI coupling around provenance, uncertainty, contingency, and bodily agency, not a new electrode or photovoltaic material.

4. **Peer-reviewed grounding.** The revision adds peer-reviewed work on smart-textile integration (Stoppa and Chiolerio, 2014) and body-worn-sensor human factors and acceptance (Bergmann and McGregor, 2011).
